# Laws of UX checks

Use these prompts to turn a law into an evidence-backed check. Choose only the checks that apply to the user task. The canonical explanations are at [Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/).

| Law or principle | Check from the evidence | Action cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fitts's Law | Are frequent or consequential targets large enough, near the expected cursor/thumb path, and separated from destructive neighbors? | Enlarge, move, or separate the target; do not rely on precision for a primary action. |
| Hick's Law / choice overload | Does the decision set contain competing, ungrouped, or equally weighted choices? | Reduce visible choices, group them, or provide a clear default and progressive disclosure. |
| Jakob's Law / mental models | Does the interaction match familiar platform and product conventions, labels, and navigation expectations? | Follow the established pattern or make the departure explicit and learnable. |
| Gestalt grouping | Do proximity, alignment, common region, similarity, and hierarchy make related controls and content legible? | Group related elements and distinguish unrelated ones with spacing, headings, or containers. |
| Cognitive-load and memory laws (including Miller's Law) | Must people remember values, instructions, location, or status across views when recognition could be supported, or process an unchunked amount of information? | Keep context visible, use recognition over recall, and chunk or reveal only task-relevant information. |
| Doherty Threshold | Does feedback begin promptly enough that people know an action registered, especially for loading or saving? | Give immediate acknowledgement and clear progress or completion state; avoid artificial delay. |
| Goal-Gradient, Peak-End, and Zeigarnik effects | Does progress toward a meaningful goal remain visible, and do interruption, completion, and the memorable end state feel clear? | Show truthful progress, preserve unfinished work, and make the completion/exit state reassuring. |
| Postel's Law / Tesler's Law | Does input accept reasonable user variation while the product carries unavoidable complexity instead of offloading it? | Be tolerant in accepted formats and simplify the workflow; do not hide validation or create ambiguity. |
| Von Restorff effect | Is visual distinction reserved for the one item that needs attention, rather than competing accents everywhere? | Make the priority item distinct and reduce competing emphasis. |

Treat these as hypotheses: confirm an issue through the provided evidence, and state uncertainty rather than manufacturing a finding.
